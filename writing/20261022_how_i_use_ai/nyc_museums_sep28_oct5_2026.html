Brooklyn Museum ↗

Suggested $20; Iris van Herpen $30
Best on Sat Oct. 3. Manga source ↗

Iris van Herpen: Sculpting the Senses pairs 140+ couture works with art, science, natural-history objects, technology, biology, and movement. A quiet art/design/fabrication wildcard: Noguchi’s stone, metal, wood, furniture, lighting, models, and sculpture are integrated with the industrial building and garden. The attraction this week is primarily the permanent collection and setting rather than a major temporary exhibition.

International Center of Photography (ICP) ↗

$18 adult

Museum at FIT ↗

Free
Figured Cloth opens Thu Oct. 1. Source ↗

Doll Dressing explores the two-way relationship between dolls and fashion, from dolls spreading new styles to modern baby-doll and paper-doll aesthetics. From Oct. 1, Figured Cloth: From Sketch to Jacquard adds student-designed jacquard textiles moving from hand-drawn motifs through weaving software into finished fabric.

Open this week: Wed 9/30, Thu 10/1, Fri 10/2, Sat 10/3, Sun 10/4

New York Transit Museum ↗

$10 adult
Open Wed–Sun 10–4. On Sat Oct. 3, 2–7pm there is a separate-ticket vintage train ride to Green-Wood Cemetery ($60 adult). Program source ↗

50 Years of Stories mines the archive for unusual experiments, proposals, and personalities from NYC transit history; FAREwell, MetroCard covers the card’s technology, rollout, graphic identity, and cultural life; Ticket to Ride traces fare collection; and the platform level contains twenty vintage subway and elevated cars dating back to 1907. The museum currently notes an A/C outage in the back mezzanine galleries and Education Center.

Open this week: Wed 9/30, Thu 10/1, Fri 10/2, Sat 10/3, Sun 10/4

Morgan Library & Museum ↗

$25 adult; Fri 5–8 free
Go by Sun Oct. 4 if you want Tarot!; that is its final day. Tarot source ↗

Tarot! Renaissance Symbols, Modern Visions traces tarot from a fifteenth-century Italian courtly card game into occult imagery and modern artistic vocabulary. Hujar: Contact uses 110+ contact sheets to reveal Peter Hujar’s shooting and editing process, while Friends Who Came to See Me focuses on drawings from John Ashbery’s collection. There is also a ticketed Morgan After Hours on Thu Oct. 1, 6–9pm. The medieval blockbook exhibition Graphic Devotions does not open until November.

Open this week: Tue 9/29, Wed 9/30, Thu 10/1, Fri 10/2, Sat 10/3, Sun 10/4

Whitney Museum of American Art ↗

$30 adult; Fri 5–10 free
Fri Oct. 2, 5–10pm is free (advance ticket recommended). The Biennial is in its final partial-view weeks. Free Friday source ↗ · Tour calendar ↗

Whitney Biennial 2026 is now only on Floor 6 and runs there through Oct. 12, with work by 56 artists, duos, and collectives responding to contemporary American life and its global entanglements. Andy Warhol Family Album presents hundreds of Polaroids from Warhol’s personal archive. Also on view are The Lost World: The Art of Minnie Evans, “Untitled” (America), and newer collection presentations. Free docent tours run most days.

Open this week: Mon 9/28, Wed 9/30, Thu 10/1, Fri 10/2, Sat 10/3, Sun 10/4, Mon 10/5

Outdoor / non-museum options

Green-Wood Cemetery

A strong non-museum choice: 19th-century landscape design, architecture, monuments, and New York history. The main gate changes from 7–7 to 8–5 on Oct. 1.

High Line

Current art includes Derek Fordjour’s painted-bronze figures on race, labor, and spectacle, Patricia Ayres’s bandaged anthropomorphic sculptures, and Alison Nguyen’s film work.

Roosevelt Island / Four Freedoms Park

Louis Kahn’s austere granite memorial uses the island’s southern tip and Manhattan skyline as part of the architecture.

Central Park

The landscape design itself is the attraction: Bethesda Terrace, the Ramble, Reservoir, North Woods, bridges, and architecture.

Brooklyn Bridge Park

Waterfront landscape, reused industrial piers, and Manhattan/bridge views.

Prospect Park

Olmsted/Vaux landscape and an easy pairing with the Brooklyn Museum.
